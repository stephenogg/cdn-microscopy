> [!IMPORTANT] Read before your first session
> This page gives general rules. Instrument-specific hazards are on each system's page. The facility's risk assessments and local rules take priority.

## In an emergency

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Injury or fire | Follow the building alarm and evacuation procedure.|
| Laser exposure to the eye or skin | Stop. Switch off the laser. Seek medical attention immediately and report it. |
| Chemical on skin or in eyes | Rinse with water for at least 15 minutes. Seek medical advice. |
| Large spill | Leave the area, warn others, contact the [facility manager](mailto:stephen.ogg@kcl.ac.uk). |
| Equipment fault, smoke, burning smell | Switch off if safe, leave it, report it. |

Facility contact: [Stephen Ogg](mailto:stephen.ogg@kcl.ac.uk).

## Lasers

Our confocal, Airyscan and STED systems use enclosed laser systems. In normal use the beam stays inside the instrument, but laser light can damage the eye, even from reflections.

Rules for all users:

1. **Never look into a beam, a lens or the scan head while lasers are on.**
2. Never remove covers, panels, interlocks or beam stops.
3. Take off watches and rings if you are working near beam paths.
4. Do not place reflective objects (tools, mirrors) near the light path.
5. Keep the sample enclosure or door closed during scanning where one is fitted.
6. Use the minimum laser power you need. This also reduces bleaching and phototoxicity.
7. Switch lasers off using the shutdown procedure for the system.
8. Report any damage, loose cover, or unusual smell or noise.

## Lamps and UV light

- Metal-halide or mercury-type lamps (HXP, X-Cite) emit UV and become very hot. Do not look into the light guide.
- Do not switch lamps off and on repeatedly, and let them cool as per the instructions. A lamp can fail violently if mishandled.
- UV excitation (about 365-405 nm) can damage skin and eyes. Don't look at the sample through the eyepieces without a filter in place.

## Chemicals

You are responsible for the safety of the samples you bring. Everything in your sample must be allowed in the facility.

### Typical hazards

| Chemical | Hazard | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Paraformaldehyde (PFA), formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde | Toxic, carcinogenic, irritant | Samples must be washed and fully fixed before arriving. Mounted slides must be sealed. |
| Mounting media (some contain phenylenediamine, DABCO, hardening media) | Irritants; some toxic | Cure fully; seal with nail varnish if the medium does not harden. |
| Immersion oil | Skin and eye irritant; slippery | Use only the oils supplied. Wipe spills. Wash hands. |
| Clearing reagents (DBE, BABB, benzyl benzoate, ethyl cinnamate, solvents) | Flammable, harmful vapour, can damage plastics and seals | Only on instruments approved for them (Blaze). Use in a fume hood for preparation; follow the cuvette and dipping-cap SOPs. Never put these on other microscopes. |
| Methanol, acetone, ethanol | Flammable, toxic (methanol) | No open containers of solvent on the instruments. |
| DMSO, sodium azide | Toxic or skin-penetrating | Declare sodium azide in solutions. |
| Dyes (DAPI, Hoechst, propidium iodide, ethidium bromide) | Suspected mutagens | Gloves; dispose as hazardous waste. |

### Biological hazards

- Samples from **human tissue, primary cells, viruses or GM organisms** must be handled according to the relevant risk assessment and containment level.
- Live samples of **Containment Level 2** or above require notification to the facility **before** booking.
- Fixed samples should be non-infectious. Say if they may not be (for example, unfixed human material).
- Never bring sharps. Use a sharps bin for broken coverslips and slides. Located at each microscope, contact the facility manager if the sharps bins are full.

### Good chemical practice

- Wear a lab coat and gloves. Remove gloves before touching shared keyboards, mice or door handles.
- Bring samples in a sealed, labelled container.
- Take **all** waste home with you unless a facility bin is designated.
- Report any spill. Do not leave it for the next user.
- Never use a chemical on the instrument that the page for that system does not allow.

## Immersion oil and water

- Use only the immersion medium specified for the objective.
- **Do not use oil on dry or water objectives.** If oil gets on the wrong lens, tell the facility rather than wiping it yourself.
- Use lens tissue only on objectives, with a small amount of lens cleaner. Never wipe a dry lens.
- Clean oil from the objective after use and wipe the stage.
- Water or buffer spills near the electronics are dangerous: switch off and report.

## Moving parts and glass

- Keep fingers and hair away from the stage and turret.
- On upright microscopes, lower the objective slowly while looking from the side. Do not focus down while looking through the eyepieces.
- Pick up broken glass only with tweezers or a brush, and dispose of it in a sharps container.
- Do not force any part of the instrument. If something jams, stop and report it.

## Housekeeping and conduct

- Don't eat or drink in the microscope room.
- Leave the bench clean and the system in its "expected state" (objective lowered away, covers on, lights off).
- Don't work alone with hazardous samples or out of hours unless you are authorised.
- Report accidents and near misses, even small ones.
